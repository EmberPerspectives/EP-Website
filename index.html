Have you ever been in the same situation as someone else and somehow walked away with a completely different experience of it? You were at the same event, with the same group, on the same night. So why did it feel so different?

Maybe nothing went wrong. Maybe no one was being difficult. But maybe you are simply different people who show up differently. You notice different things. You need different things. What feels obvious to you might not have even registered for the person sitting right beside you.

Ember Perspectives started by asking: What makes people experience the same situation so differently?

Same Campfire. Different Experiences.

Why a Campfire?

It started with two camping trips. One with family and another with friends.

As I started planning, I began noticing how differently everyone approached the same things. People had their own ways of prepping and preparing. Activities that would recharge some people would completely drain others. Some wanted structure and a plan, while others were perfectly happy to see what happened. Some needed quiet time to feel restored. Others felt most energized with the group around them.

But at the end of every night, no matter how differently the day had unfolded, everyone ended up around the same campfire.

The fire was the same. The experiences around it were not.

That observation stayed with me. I started seeing the same pattern beyond those camping trips: in relationships, at work, and in everyday life.

I've always been curious about other people's perspectives. How can the same moment, interaction, or thing register so differently from one person to another? My background in Industrial-Organizational Psychology gave me a research lens for exploring that question. Rather than creating my own answer, I wanted to build from something already grounded in personality research.

What began as curiosity about people I love eventually became Ember Perspectives.

What Ember Perspectives Became

A symbolic framework for understanding what we naturally bring to shared experiences.

Ember Perspectives uses the metaphor of a campfire to explore how people show up differently in the spaces they share. Each person carries five symbolic tools, each representing one of the five broad dimensions of personality in the Big Five model. The Big Five is one of the most extensively studied frameworks in personality psychology.

These tools aren't types. They're not boxes. They're not fixed roles.

Everyone carries all five.

You are not the flashlight. You have a flashlight. So does everyone else.

What Shapes the Fire?

Knowing which tools we all carry is only the beginning.

Our tools have parts and natural ranges, but they don't show up exactly the same way all the time. Current capacity matters. The environment matters. Who else is around the fire matters. Ember Perspectives looks at three layers together to understand the bigger picture.

Layer 1: The Five Tools

What everyone brings. Each tool is made up of individual parts, each with its own spectrum and natural range.

Layer 2: Tool Conditions

The same tool performs differently depending on its current state. Full batteries or drained batteries can change everything.

Layer 3: The Fire Pit

The context around the fire that shapes which contributions feel welcomed, safe, or possible.

New Perspective. New Language.

The Research Foundation

Ember Perspectives is grounded in the Big Five model of personality, one of the most extensively studied frameworks in personality psychology.

Decades of research across different populations and cultures have found support for these broad dimensions of personality. Unlike personality systems that sort people into fixed types, the Big Five measures traits along continuous dimensions, recognizing that people fall somewhere along a spectrum rather than into one category or another.

The Big Five describes five broad dimensions: Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Neuroticism, Agreeableness, and Openness to Experience.

Ember Perspectives doesn't replace that research. It translates it.

The five tools bring those dimensions into everyday life, giving us a more familiar way to recognize and talk about the differences between us. They also give us language for the full spectrum, making it easier to see how different ways of showing up can change the fire without treating one end as the "right" one.

The research provides the foundation. The campfire gives us a way to visualize and understand it.

Grounded in real research, translated for real life.
